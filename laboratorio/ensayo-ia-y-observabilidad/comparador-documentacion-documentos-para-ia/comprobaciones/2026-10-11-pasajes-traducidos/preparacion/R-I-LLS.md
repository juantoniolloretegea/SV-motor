# R-I-LLS

## P-I02

• Hairy cell leukemia (HCL) is a rare, slow-growing leukemia that begins in a B cell (also called a B lymphocyte), a type of white blood cell. The disease is also called hairy cell leukemia and hairy cell leukemia.

## P-I03

Hairy cell leukemia is usually slow-growing, and not all patients need to begin treatment immediately after initial diagnosis. In approximately 10 percent of patients (who have stable blood cell counts and no symptoms at diagnosis), the treatment approach may be watchful waiting. Watchful waiting is an appropriate medical approach that involves delaying treatment until signs and symptoms of the disease appear or progress. Some patients with hairy cell leukemia live for many years without any symptoms and without receiving any treatment. Frequent monitoring of the patient, including blood tests, is necessary so that treatment can be initiated if the disease begins to progress.

## P-I04

Patients should begin treatment if they have low blood cell counts (deficiency of red blood cells, platelets, or white blood cells). They should also begin treatment if they experience symptoms, including unexplained weight loss, recurrent infections, or physical discomfort due to an enlarged spleen and/or liver.
